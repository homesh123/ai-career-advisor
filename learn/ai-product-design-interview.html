🏗️ AI product system design: designing an AI feature end to end

EnglishHinglish

🏠 Real-life example

An architect asked to design a house doesn't start drawing the kitchen tiles. They ask who will live there, the budget and the plot size, sketch the rooms and how people move between them, then talk about plumbing, safety and what happens in the monsoon. An AI design interview rewards the same order: users and goal first, then the flow, then the hard parts and how you'll know it works.

💡 In simple words

In this round you get a prompt such as "Design an AI assistant that answers customer questions from our help centre" and 45 minutes. A structure that works: (1) Clarify: who the users are, what job they need done, scale (users, documents, languages), and what success means in numbers. Ask whether AI is even needed. (2) User experience: where the feature appears, what the user sees while waiting, how they verify and correct it, and the failure path. (3) Architecture: client, API, the model wrapper, retrieval (ingestion, chunking, hybrid search, reranking), tools, storage, and background jobs; draw boxes and arrows. (4) Model and prompt choices with reasons: which model for which step, structured output where code consumes the result. (5) Quality: an offline eval set, launch criteria, online signals and the feedback loop. (6) Production: latency budget per step, cost per request, caching, rate limits, fallbacks, privacy, permissions and prompt injection. (7) Rollout and iteration: canary, A/B test, what you would build in version two. Talk in trade-offs ("a smaller model saves 70% cost; I'd accept it if evals stay within 2 points") and keep checking time with the interviewer.

🎬 See how it works

A 45 minute plan for the AI design round: clarify, user experience, architecture, model choices, quality, production limits, then rollout. Talk in trade-offs with numbers.

🪜 Step by step

  1. Spend the first 5 minutes on clarifying questions: users, job to be done, scale, languages, constraints and success metric.
  2. Describe the user flow: entry point, what streams, how users verify and correct, and the failure path.
  3. Draw the architecture: client, API, model wrapper, retrieval pipeline, tools, storage, queue; walk one request through it.
  4. Choose models per step with reasons, and say where you use structured output and where you stream.
  5. Explain quality: eval set, launch criteria, online signals, feedback loop.
  6. Cover production: latency budget, cost per request, caching, limits, fallbacks, privacy, permissions and prompt injection.
  7. Finish with rollout and a version two plan, and summarise your key trade-offs in one minute.

🔑 Key terms

Clarifying questions
Questions about users, scale and success that shape the whole design.
Latency budget
Splitting the allowed response time across steps (retrieval 150 ms, model 1.5 s…).
Launch criteria
The eval and safety thresholds that must be met before release.
Trade-off
Stating what you gain and what you give up with each choice.

⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

  • Jumping straight to a model name and a vector database before understanding the user and goal.
  • No word on evaluation. "How will you know it works?" is the question interviewers care about most.
  • Ignoring cost and latency, or giving no numbers at all.

The interviewer says "Design an AI feature that summarises long customer calls." What should you do first?

  2. Draw the full architecture with every service

🙋 Questions learners ask

How deep should I go on model internals?

For product roles, only as far as it affects decisions: context limits, latency, cost, reliability of formats. Spend the time on UX, quality and trade-offs.

How do I practise this round?

Pick 10 everyday AI features (email reply drafts, meeting notes, search, a shopping assistant), set a 45 minute timer, talk through the 7 parts out loud and draw the diagram. Review against this lesson.

🎯 Asked in interviews on this topic

Try answering out loud first, then open the model answer.

Design an AI "summarise this thread" feature for a team chat app.

💬

Clarify: thread length, languages, latency target and privacy rules. Flow: when the user clicks summarise, the backend fetches the messages they can see, trims or chunks very long threads, and calls the LLM with a prompt asking for decisions, open questions and action items with owners, in a structured format. Stream the result into the UI with a "draft" label and links to the source messages. Cache summaries and update them incrementally when new messages arrive. Evaluate with a set of real threads, scored for missed decisions and invented facts. Track usage, thumbs down and cost per summary.

✅

  • Clarify constraints
  • Permission aware fetching, chunking long threads
  • Structured prompt; streaming UI with source links
  • Caching, evals, metrics

📌 Remember

Clarify, then UX, architecture, quality, production limits and rollout, always explaining trade-offs with numbers.
